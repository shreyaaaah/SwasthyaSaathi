SECTION 1: HYPERTENSION (HIGH BLOOD PRESSURE)
=======================================================================

WHAT IS HYPERTENSION?
Blood pressure is recorded as two numbers: Systolic (top) / Diastolic (bottom), measured in mmHg.

Blood Pressure Categories (Adults):
- Normal: Below 120/80 mmHg
- Elevated: 120–129 / below 80 mmHg
- Stage 1 Hypertension: 130–139 / 80–89 mmHg
- Stage 2 Hypertension: 140/90 mmHg or higher
- Hypertensive Crisis (Emergency): 180/120 mmHg or higher — seek emergency care immediately

SYMPTOMS
Hypertension is often called the "silent killer" because most people have NO symptoms. When symptoms occur, they may include:
- Headache (especially at the back of the head, in the morning)
- Dizziness or lightheadedness
- Blurred vision
- Nosebleeds (in severe hypertension)

Hypertensive Emergency Signs (Seek Emergency Care — Call 112/108):
- Blood pressure above 180/120 mmHg
- Severe headache with blurred vision or chest pain
- Difficulty breathing, seizures, or confusion

LIFESTYLE MODIFICATIONS (All stages of hypertension)
1. DIET — DASH Approach (Dietary Approaches to Stop Hypertension):
   - Reduce salt (sodium): Target less than 5g per day (about 1 teaspoon). Avoid pickles, papad, processed foods, and salted snacks.
   - Increase potassium-rich foods: bananas, sweet potatoes, spinach, lentils (dal), tomatoes
   - Eat more fruits, vegetables, and whole grains
   - Reduce saturated fat: avoid vanaspati, excessive ghee, full-fat dairy in large amounts

2. PHYSICAL ACTIVITY:
   - At least 150 minutes per week of moderate aerobic exercise (brisk walking, cycling, swimming)
   - Aim for 30 minutes most days of the week

3. WEIGHT MANAGEMENT:
   - Every 1 kg reduction in body weight lowers BP by approximately 1 mmHg
   - Target BMI: 18.5–22.9 kg/m² for Indians

4. TOBACCO CESSATION:
   - Smoking raises BP acutely and is a major risk factor for heart attack and stroke
   - Free tobacco cessation support: iCare helpline 1800-11-2356

5. ALCOHOL REDUCTION:
   - Limit to no more than 1 standard drink per day (women) or 2 per day (men)
   - Alcohol raises BP and reduces the effectiveness of BP medications

MEDICATION
- If lifestyle changes are insufficient, antihypertensive medications are prescribed by a doctor
- Common first-line medicines in India: Amlodipine, Losartan, Hydrochlorothiazide, Atenolol
- CRITICAL: Do NOT stop BP medication suddenly without consulting your doctor — it can cause a dangerous rebound rise in blood pressure
- Take medication at the same time every day; use a pill organiser or mobile reminder
- Free antihypertensives are available under the Jan Aushadhi Scheme and at government PHCs

MONITORING AT HOME
- Check blood pressure at rest after sitting quietly for 5 minutes
- Check at the same time each day (morning before medication is ideal)
- Keep a record to share with your doctor

SECTION 2: DIABETES (TYPE 2) MANAGEMENT
=======================================================================

WHAT IS DIABETES?
Type 2 Diabetes is a chronic condition where blood sugar (glucose) remains too high because the body cannot use insulin properly.

Diagnostic Criteria:
- Fasting blood glucose: 126 mg/dL or above (on two separate days)
- Random blood glucose: 200 mg/dL or above with symptoms
- HbA1c: 6.5% or above
- Pre-diabetes: Fasting glucose 100–125 mg/dL; HbA1c 5.7–6.4%

SYMPTOMS OF HIGH BLOOD SUGAR (Hyperglycemia)
- Increased thirst and frequent urination
- Fatigue and weakness
- Blurred vision
- Slow healing of cuts or wounds
- Frequent infections (skin, urinary tract)
- Tingling or numbness in feet or hands

LIFESTYLE MANAGEMENT (Cornerstone of Diabetes Control)
1. DIET:
   - Control portion sizes; avoid skipping meals
   - Choose complex carbohydrates: whole grains (brown rice, millets, whole wheat), lentils
   - Avoid refined carbohydrates: maida, white rice (large portions), sweet beverages, packaged snacks
   - Increase dietary fibre: vegetables, fruits (in moderate portions), legumes
   - Healthy Indian food choices: dal, sabzi, roti (whole wheat), curd (plain), buttermilk
   - Foods to limit: rice in large amounts, potato, fruit juices, jaggery, sweets, alcohol

2. PHYSICAL ACTIVITY:
   - 150 minutes of moderate exercise per week (30 min/day, 5 days a week)
   - Walking after meals (15–20 minutes) significantly reduces post-meal blood sugar spikes
   - Resistance/strength exercises twice a week improve insulin sensitivity

3. WEIGHT MANAGEMENT:
   - Even a 5–7% reduction in body weight can significantly improve blood sugar levels in overweight individuals

MEDICATION
- Common first-line medication: Metformin (taken with meals to reduce side effects)
- Other medicines: Glipizide, Glimepiride, DPP-4 inhibitors, SGLT2 inhibitors — prescribed based on individual assessment
- Some patients require insulin injections; this is NOT a failure — it is a medical decision based on the body's needs
- Do NOT adjust or stop diabetes medication without consulting your doctor

MONITORING BLOOD SUGAR AT HOME
- Fasting glucose (before breakfast): Target 80–130 mg/dL
- Post-meal glucose (2 hours after eating): Target below 180 mg/dL
- HbA1c every 3–6 months: Target below 7% (individualised by your doctor)

RECOGNISING AND MANAGING HYPOGLYCEMIA (LOW BLOOD SUGAR)
Blood sugar below 70 mg/dL is hypoglycemia — this is a medical emergency.

Symptoms: Trembling, sweating, rapid heartbeat, dizziness, confusion, pale skin, sudden hunger
Treatment (Rule of 15):
- Eat 15g of fast-acting carbohydrates: 3–4 glucose tablets, 1/2 cup (125ml) of fruit juice or regular (not diet) soda, or 1 tablespoon of sugar dissolved in water
- Wait 15 minutes; if symptoms persist, repeat
- Once recovered, eat a small snack (biscuit + milk)
- If unconscious — DO NOT give anything by mouth; call 108 immediately

FOOT CARE FOR DIABETICS
- Inspect feet DAILY for cuts, blisters, redness, or swelling
- Wash feet daily in warm (not hot) water and dry gently between toes
- Wear well-fitting, closed footwear — never walk barefoot
- See a doctor immediately for any foot wound, ulcer, or infection (diabetic foot infections can worsen rapidly)

PREVENTING COMPLICATIONS
Uncontrolled diabetes damages blood vessels and nerves over time. Regular check-ups:
- Eye check (retinal exam): Every 1–2 years
- Kidney function (urine microalbumin, creatinine): Every year
- Foot examination: Every visit
- Lipid profile and BP check: Every visit

FREE DIABETES CARE RESOURCES IN INDIA
- Free testing and medicines at government PHCs and CHCs under NP-NCD
- Jan Aushadhi Kendras: Metformin, Glimepiride, insulin at reduced cost
- NHP helpline: 1800-11-4000

SECTION 3: HYPERTENSION + DIABETES TOGETHER (DUAL RISK)
=======================================================================

Having both hypertension and diabetes significantly increases the risk of heart attack, stroke, and kidney disease.
- Blood pressure target for diabetics: Below 130/80 mmHg
- Use of an ACE inhibitor or ARB (e.g., Ramipril, Losartan) is preferred in diabetics with hypertension — protects the kidneys
- Strict HbA1c control (below 7%) along with BP control is essential
- Annual urine albumin test is critical to detect early kidney damage

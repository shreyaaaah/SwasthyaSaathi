SECTION 1: WHEN TO CALL EMERGENCY SERVICES IMMEDIATELY (112 / 108)
=======================================================================

Call emergency services (112 or 108 ambulance) WITHOUT DELAY if you or someone around you experiences ANY of the following:

CHEST PAIN OR PRESSURE
- Sudden, severe pain, tightness, squeezing, crushing, or pressure in the centre or left side of the chest
- Chest discomfort that lasts more than a few minutes or comes and goes
- Chest pain spreading or radiating to the arm (especially left arm), shoulder, jaw, neck, or back
- Chest pain accompanied by sweating (diaphoresis), nausea, or vomiting
- Chest pain with shortness of breath even at rest
- Chest pain in a person with known heart disease, diabetes, or hypertension

BREATHING DIFFICULTY (DYSPNEA)
- Sudden severe shortness of breath at rest (not caused by exercise)
- Unable to complete a sentence due to difficulty breathing
- Breathing with audible wheezing, gurgling, or gasping sounds
- Bluish discolouration of lips, fingertips, or face (cyanosis)

LOSS OF CONSCIOUSNESS / COLLAPSE
- Sudden fainting or loss of consciousness
- Unresponsiveness — person cannot be woken up
- Seizures with no prior history of epilepsy

STROKE SYMPTOMS — REMEMBER "FAST"
- FACE: Sudden drooping on one side of the face; uneven smile
- ARM: Sudden weakness or numbness in one arm; inability to raise both arms equally
- SPEECH: Sudden slurred or garbled speech; unable to speak or understand
- TIME: If any of the above — Time to call 112 immediately
- Additional stroke signs: sudden severe headache with no cause, sudden vision loss in one or both eyes, sudden loss of balance or coordination

HEART ATTACK (MYOCARDIAL INFARCTION) KEY SIGNS
- Chest pain or pressure lasting more than 15–20 minutes
- Cold sweat, paleness, and extreme weakness
- Feeling of impending doom or extreme anxiety
- Jaw pain or toothache-like pain with no dental cause
- Pain between the shoulder blades
Note: In women, symptoms may be atypical — fatigue, nausea, upper abdominal pain, and jaw pain without classic chest pain.

=======================================================================
SECTION 2: WHAT TO DO WHILE WAITING FOR THE AMBULANCE
=======================================================================

1. CALL 112 or 108 — state clearly "possible heart attack" or "chest pain" and your location.
2. Keep the person CALM and at REST — do not allow them to walk or exert themselves.
3. Loosen tight clothing (collar, belt, tie) to help breathing.
4. If the person is conscious, and not allergic — give ONE tablet of Aspirin 325mg (or two 150mg tablets) to chew (not swallow whole), ONLY if available and not contraindicated.
5. If the person is unconscious and not breathing — begin CPR (Cardiopulmonary Resuscitation) if you are trained.
6. Do NOT give food, water, or any other medication.
7. Do NOT leave the person alone.
8. Note the time symptoms started — this information is critical for emergency staff.

=======================================================================
SECTION 3: HIGH-RISK GROUPS FOR HEART ATTACK AND CARDIAC EVENTS
=======================================================================

The following groups have significantly higher risk and should seek evaluation even for mild or atypical symptoms:
- Age above 45 years (men) or above 55 years (women)
- Known hypertension (high blood pressure) or diabetes
- Known coronary artery disease or previous heart attack
- Smokers or tobacco users (current or former)
- Obesity (BMI > 30 kg/m²) or abdominal obesity
- Family history of heart disease at a young age
- Chronic kidney disease or dyslipidemia (abnormal cholesterol)

These individuals should NOT wait — seek emergency care for any chest discomfort, even if mild.

=======================================================================
SECTION 4: NON-EMERGENCY CHEST PAIN — CONSULT DOCTOR SOON
=======================================================================

Not all chest pain is cardiac. The following patterns suggest non-emergency causes but still require a doctor's evaluation within 24–48 hours:

- Sharp, stabbing pain that worsens only when you press on the chest wall (costochondritis / musculoskeletal)
- Pain clearly related to breathing in and out (pleurisy)
- Burning behind the breastbone after meals (GERD / acid reflux) — typically relieved by antacids
- Brief, fleeting pain lasting only 1–2 seconds
- Chest tightness with a productive cough and fever (likely respiratory infection)

Important: If you are unsure whether your chest pain is cardiac or non-cardiac, ALWAYS treat it as a potential emergency and seek evaluation. It is always safer to be evaluated and reassured than to ignore a cardiac event.

=======================================================================
SECTION 5: CHEST PAIN IN CHILDREN
=======================================================================

Chest pain in children is usually non-cardiac (most often musculoskeletal or related to cough and respiratory infection). However, seek emergency care if a child has:
- Chest pain during exertion (running, sports)
- Chest pain with fainting or near-fainting
- Chest pain with palpitations or an irregular heartbeat
- Known congenital heart disease

=======================================================================
SECTION 6: NATIONAL HELPLINES AND EMERGENCY RESOURCES
=======================================================================

- Emergency Ambulance: 108 (free, nationwide)
- National Emergency Number: 112
- Chest pain / cardiac care — go to the nearest government hospital with a 24-hour emergency or ICU. In India, government hospitals under the PM Jan Arogya Yojana (Ayushman Bharat) provide free emergency cardiac care for eligible beneficiaries.
- National Helpline for NCD (Non-Communicable Diseases including heart disease): 1800-11-4000 (Toll-free)

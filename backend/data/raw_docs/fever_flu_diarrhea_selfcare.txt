SECTION 1: FEVER — SELF-CARE AND WHEN TO SEE A DOCTOR
=======================================================================

WHAT IS FEVER?
Normal body temperature is 37°C (98.6°F). A temperature of 38°C (100.4°F) or above is considered a fever.

SELF-CARE FOR MILD FEVER (38°C–38.9°C / 100.4°F–102°F)
- Rest and drink plenty of fluids (water, oral rehydration solution, coconut water, clear soups)
- Wear light, comfortable clothing; use a light blanket only if shivering
- Apply a cool, damp cloth to the forehead, armpits, and neck for comfort (do not use very cold water or ice)
- Paracetamol (Acetaminophen): 500mg–1000mg for adults every 4–6 hours (not more than 4g per day) — safe and effective for reducing fever
  - For children: weight-based dosing (15mg/kg per dose, every 4–6 hours)
  - Do NOT give Aspirin to children under 18 years
- Avoid strenuous activity

WHEN TO SEE A DOCTOR (Consult Within 24 Hours)
- Fever above 39.5°C (103°F) not responding to paracetamol after 1 hour
- Fever lasting more than 3 days in adults or more than 2 days in young children
- Fever in an infant under 3 months — ALWAYS seek medical evaluation
- Fever with severe headache and neck stiffness (possible meningitis — seek emergency care)
- Fever with a rash, especially a red, non-blanching rash
- Fever with confusion or altered mental state (possible encephalitis)
- Fever with severe abdominal pain or back pain
- Fever in a returned traveller (possible malaria, typhoid, dengue)
- Fever in a person with diabetes, heart disease, or immunocompromised conditions

DENGUE WARNING SIGNS WITH FEVER — SEEK EMERGENCY CARE
- Severe abdominal pain or persistent vomiting
- Bleeding from gums or nose, blood in urine or stool
- Sudden drop in fever with extreme weakness, pale cold skin (shock)
- Difficulty breathing

SECTION 2: COMMON COLD AND INFLUENZA (FLU) — SELF-CARE
=======================================================================

SYMPTOMS
- Common Cold: Runny/blocked nose, sneezing, sore throat, mild cough, low-grade fever
- Influenza (Flu): Sudden high fever (39–40°C), severe body aches, fatigue, headache, dry cough, sore throat

SELF-CARE (Both Common Cold and Flu)
- REST — one of the most important treatments
- FLUIDS — drink 8–10 glasses of water per day; warm water with honey and lemon can soothe the throat
- STEAM INHALATION — 2–3 times per day for congestion (take care to avoid burns)
- SALT WATER GARGLE — 1/4 to 1/2 teaspoon of salt in warm water, gargle 3 times daily
- HONEY — 1–2 teaspoons before sleep can help reduce cough (not for infants under 1 year)
- NASAL DROPS — normal saline nasal drops can relieve congestion in children safely
- PARACETAMOL — for fever and body aches; follow dosing as above

INFLUENZA VACCINATION
The seasonal influenza vaccine is recommended annually. High-priority groups include:
- Elderly (above 65 years)
- Pregnant women
- Young children aged 6 months to 5 years
- Healthcare workers
- Persons with chronic illness (diabetes, heart disease, asthma)

WHEN TO SEEK MEDICAL ATTENTION (Flu Complications)
- Difficulty breathing or rapid breathing
- Persistent chest pain or pressure
- Confusion or altered consciousness
- Severe or persistent vomiting (unable to keep fluids down)
- Symptoms improve then return with fever and worsening cough
- Lips or face turning bluish (cyanosis)
- High-risk groups: infants, elderly, pregnant women, and immunocompromised — should seek care sooner

PREVENTION
- Wash hands frequently with soap and water for 20 seconds
- Cover mouth and nose with a bent elbow or tissue when coughing or sneezing
- Avoid close contact with sick people
- Avoid touching your eyes, nose, and mouth

SECTION 3: DIARRHEA — SELF-CARE AND WHEN TO SEEK CARE
=======================================================================

WHAT IS DIARRHEA?
Passing 3 or more loose or liquid stools per day. Most acute diarrhea in adults is self-limiting and resolves within 2–3 days.

THE MOST IMPORTANT TREATMENT: ORAL REHYDRATION
Diarrhea causes loss of water and salts. The primary risk is DEHYDRATION.

ORS (Oral Rehydration Solution) — HOW TO PREPARE AT HOME
- 1 litre of clean boiled water
- 6 level teaspoons of sugar
- 1/2 teaspoon of salt
- Mix well and drink throughout the day

Alternatively, use packaged ORS sachets (available free at government health centres and Anganwadi centres).

FEEDING DURING DIARRHEA
- Adults and older children: continue eating normally — bland foods like rice, curd, banana, boiled potatoes
- Infants: continue breastfeeding; do NOT stop breast milk during diarrhea
- Avoid: spicy food, oily food, raw vegetables, sugary drinks, fruit juices (these worsen diarrhea)

ZINC FOR CHILDREN (WHO/UNICEF Recommendation)
- Children 6 months–5 years with diarrhea: Zinc 20mg/day for 14 days (10mg/day for infants under 6 months)
- Available free at government PHCs; reduces severity and duration of diarrhea

WHEN TO SEEK MEDICAL CARE URGENTLY
- Signs of severe dehydration: sunken eyes, dry mouth, no urination for 6+ hours, very dark urine, extreme thirst, skin tenting (skin stays pinched when pulled)
- Bloody or mucoid diarrhea (red, black, or tarry stools)
- Fever above 39°C with diarrhea
- Diarrhea lasting more than 3 days without improvement
- Vomiting with inability to keep ORS down
- Diarrhea in an infant under 6 months
- In children: high-pitched cry, inability to drink, prolonged sleep, not waking up

PREVENTION
- Drink only clean, boiled, or purified water
- Wash hands before eating and after using the toilet
- Keep food covered; avoid street food in monsoon season
- Immunise children against Rotavirus (free under Universal Immunisation Programme — UIP)
